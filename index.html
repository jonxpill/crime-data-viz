MISDAADVELD

Western Cape crime as a field of light · Afrikaans: “crime field” · 2008/09 – 2025/26

Every glowing dot is one reported crime. Eighteen years of South African Police Service records for the Western Cape — robbery, residential burglary, murder, sexual offences, commercial crime and carjacking across 150 police precincts — drawn as a single living field of ~180,000 points. The province, its districts, the pies, the terrain and the monthly pulse are all the same dots rearranging; nothing is ever redrawn, only moved.

Glow is crime; grey is geography. Colour is local density — cool and sparse through molten and dense. Click a district to bloom into it. N breathes month by month, C shows rates per 100k people, T raises the land itself. H stands you in your suburb: the field dims to your precinct and its residents rise as grey dots, one per hundred people — count your neighbours per burglary.

Honesty notes. Counts are real; positions are jittered within each precinct (volume is true, street addresses are not). Raw counts largely follow population — per-capita (C) is the fairer read (denominators: WorldPop 2020). 2025/26 is the sum of SAPS’s quarterly releases and is unaudited; the monthly series (Apr 2021 – Mar 2026) comes from the same quarterlies. Stations opened after 2023 (Samora Machel, Makhaza) are folded into the older precinct boundaries that contain them. Carjacking is also a subcategory of aggravated robbery, so it appears inside the robbery lens too. And throughout: these are reported crimes — what reaches a police station, not all that happens.

The unlit field (U). On any map view, U condenses a second population out of the dark: slate-violet dots that never glow — an estimate of the crimes that never reached a police station. Per precinct-year it draws R×(1−r)/r unlit dots beside the R reported ones, where r is the national reporting rate from Stats SA's Governance, Public Safety and Justice Survey (GPSJS 2024/25, cross-checked against 2023/24): housebreaking 43,4% · street robbery 36,3% · sexual offences 56,3% · hijacking 76,8%. The rate is national and applied uniformly — the true geography of underreporting is unknown, so the estimate carries the reported field's shape by construction. Sexual offences are a floor: surveys themselves under-capture them, so the true unlit count is higher than drawn. Murder (near-fully recorded) and commercial crime (outside household surveys) get no estimate — excluded, never guessed.
